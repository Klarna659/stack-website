Why not just use a shot tracker

Most of these apps track one drug. You are not taking one drug.

A GLP-1 tracker handles the pen. It has nothing to say about the capsules at breakfast, the peptide at nine, or the magnesium at ten — and no idea that three of them overlap. Stack starts from the assumption that there are six.

What that buys you

Three things a spreadsheet will never do.

01 / SUPPLY

It knows you go dry on the 21st.

Every logged dose draws the vial down, so the refill date arrives three weeks early instead of on the morning you needed it.

02 / ROTATION

It remembers which side you used.

A body map keeps the rotation honest, greys out what is still resting, and hands you the next site.

03 / LEVELS

It shows what has not cleared yet.

A relative curve modelled from published half-lives, so overlapping doses stop being something you carry in your head. A model, not a measurement.

The edges

It will not tell you what to take.

Stack records and explains. It will not suggest a compound, recommend a dose, or read a lab result and pronounce on it. The curve is a model built from published half-lives, not a measurement of your blood. Nothing in the app or on this site is medical advice, and the one thing we will always tell you to do is talk to a clinician. We would rather you knew that here than on the fourth screen.
