Stack · mark, round 3

Five more directions, drawn from new territory, none of it round 2's.

Round 2's verdict was not a rejection — "none is a for-sure yet... keep those, generate five more, maybe one will grow on me." So all five round-2 marks stay on file exactly as drawn, undecided. This sheet adds five new directions — a tally mark, a staircase, a hand-built S, a row of dots, a folded page — none of them pills, capsules, horizontal stacked bars, or gradients, and runs each through the same four gates as everything before it.

One colourEvery mark is currentColor only — no gradient, no second value, no opacity trick standing in for one.
Legible at 16pxThe favicon size. Two of the five new directions (09, and honestly 06) are the ones most likely to fail it — both are rendered here twice, on both grounds, at all four sizes.
Names no substanceNo pill, no syringe, no vial. Only round 2's Capsule S breaks this rule, on file and on purpose; none of the five new directions go near it.
Borrows no silhouetteNo horizontal stacked bars (Stack Overflow's mark), no gradient or AI-blob fill. New for this round, because the brief named both explicitly.
What round 2 actually said, verbatim: "none is a for-sure yet... keep those, generate five more." Nothing below replaces 01–05 — they're carried into the closing strip on this page unchanged, so all ten can be looked at side by side, once, in one place.
06

Tally

the counting gesture, drawn straight

What it means: four strokes and a fifth crossing them — the oldest counting mark there is, doing exactly what a dose log does: one mark added at a time, day after day, no metaphor required. Why it survives small: every stroke is the same weight and the same square cap, so at 16px it either reads as an even, deliberate hatch or it doesn't — there's no curve or fill to soften a bad reduction. Risk: this is the direction most likely to blur into a single dark smear below about 20px, and it's the least authored of the ten — it borrows a pre-existing counting convention rather than inventing a shape of its own.

07

Titration Stairs

one continuous ascent, drawn as steps, not a slope

What it means: three risers drawn as a single unbroken stroke, climbing right — the product's own titration mechanic, a dose that steps up over weeks, as a silhouette. Why it survives small: it's one path, one stroke weight, mitred square corners — the same "nothing to blur" logic as 05 Mass, just angular instead of curved. Risk: at 16px the three steps can fuse into a blocky zigzag that reads as a bar chart or a capital M before it reads as stairs; it also sits close enough to 01 Ascending Threshold in concept that shipping both would read as indecision, not range.

08

S Monogram

an authored letterform, not a typeset one

What it means: a custom S built on a grid, not lifted from a font — two curves related by an exact 180-degree rotation about the mark's own centre, so both terminals are cut at the identical angle by construction, not by eye. Why it survives small: one continuous stroke of even weight with a tight aperture, closer in spirit to the lettermarks on Whoop or Eight Sleep than to a wordmark's initial. Risk: a monogram carries the least product meaning of the ten — it says "this is our S," not "this is what we do" — and a hand-built S this close to a real font's S invites the exact "doesn't look professional" critique that killed the ellipses, if the curve isn't tuned past this first pass.

09

Seven Dots

the week, with today logged

What it means: seven days in a row, six open and one solid — the daily rhythm the whole app runs on, drawn as literally as a calendar strip without becoming one. Why it survives small: it doesn't, not at seven — below about 64px it switches to mk-dots5, a dedicated five-dot redraw with the same open/filled logic, which is the honest answer to "test hard at 16px" rather than a workaround. Risk: it's the only one of the ten that needs two different drawings to work across sizes — a real maintenance cost — and a row of circles risks reading as a loading indicator or a carousel control before it reads as tracking.

10

The Fold

a kept page, corner turned down

What it means: one bold card with a corner missing — the negative space itself is the fold, a record that's been touched and dog-eared rather than a blank template. Why it survives small: it's a single filled shape with no internal line, gap, or knockout to lose, which makes it — with 05 Mass — the most durable of all ten marks under reduction. Risk: without an internal crease line, deliberately omitted for that small-size durability, it can read at larger sizes as simply "a square with a cut corner" or a bookmark rather than specifically a fold; the idea is legible but generic at rest.
