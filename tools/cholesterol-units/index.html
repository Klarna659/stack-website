Switch cholesterol and triglyceride results between mg/dL and mmol/L. Each has its own factor: 38.67 for cholesterol, 88.57 for triglycerides.

How the numbers are worked out

The factor for each substance comes from its molar mass, the weight of one mole in grams. One millimole per litre is that many milligrams per litre, and a decilitre is a tenth of a litre, so the molar mass divided by 10 gives the factor in mg/dL.

  • Total, LDL and HDL cholesterol: 1 mmol/L = 38.67 mg/dL.
  • Triglycerides: 1 mmol/L = 88.57 mg/dL.

To go from mg/dL to mmol/L, divide by the factor. To go from mmol/L to mg/dL, multiply by it. Each kind keeps its own factor. Dividing a triglyceride result by 38.67 instead of 88.57 gives 3.36 instead of 1.47 for 130 mg/dL.

Questions

Do total, LDL and HDL cholesterol use the same factor?

Yes. All three are reported as cholesterol, so they share the factor 38.67. Triglycerides are a different substance and use 88.57.

Why is the triglyceride factor different?

The factor comes from the molar mass of the substance. Cholesterol is about 386.65 grams per mole. Triglycerides are a family of molecules, and 885.7 grams per mole is the figure normally used for them, which gives 88.57.

Can I use the glucose factor for these?

No. Each substance has its own factor. Using the wrong one gives an answer that is wrong by a large amount, not a rounding error. The glucose converter uses 18.016.

Does this say whether my result is good or bad?

No. This page converts units and nothing else. It gives no ranges and no interpretation. Your clinician or pharmacist can tell you what a result means for you.
