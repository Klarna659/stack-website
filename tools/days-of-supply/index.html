How the numbers are worked out

Doses on hand is the amount you have divided by the amount you take each time, rounded down. Days of supply is that number of doses divided by how many you take a day, rounded down to whole days. Sixty tablets at one each, twice a day, is 30 days.

If you take something every few days, each dose counts as that many days instead. Ten doses taken once every 3 days is 30 days.

Runs out is the first date you would not have a full day covered. Reorder by is that date minus your lead time. The date counts from the day you enter, so change it if you have already started.

Working from a vial of liquid? The vial run-out calculator handles mg, mL and dead space.

Not medical advice. This calculator counts what you have and how fast you use it. It does not say how much to take. Follow your prescriber or the product label, and ask your pharmacist about refills.
