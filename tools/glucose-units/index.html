How the numbers are worked out

Glucose has a molar mass of 180.16 grams per mole, so one millimole is 180.16 milligrams. A litre holds ten decilitres, so one millimole per litre is 18.016 milligrams per decilitre. That gives the single factor behind both directions: 1 mmol/L = 18.016 mg/dL.

To go from mg/dL to mmol/L, divide by 18.016. To go from mmol/L to mg/dL, multiply by 18.016. Some countries report one unit and some the other, so you may see both on meters, apps and reports.

This page converts the unit and nothing else. It gives no ranges and does not say what a number means.

Questions

Why 18.016 and not 18?

Glucose has a molar mass of 180.16 grams per mole, which gives 18.016. Many sources round it to 18. The two agree to within about 0.1 percent, so 18 is close enough for a quick check, but 18.016 is the exact figure.

Which way do I convert?

To go from mg/dL to mmol/L, divide by 18.016. To go from mmol/L to mg/dL, multiply by 18.016. Check the unit printed next to the number on your meter or report before you convert.

Is the same factor used for cholesterol?

No. Each substance has its own factor because the factor comes from the molar mass. See the cholesterol and triglycerides converter, which uses a different number for each.

Does this say whether my reading is high or low?

No. This page converts units and nothing else. It gives no ranges and no interpretation. Your clinician or pharmacist can tell you what a result means for you.
