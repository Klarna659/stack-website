How the numbers are worked out

Each dose fades on its own first-order curve: half of it is gone after one half-life, half of what is left after the next, and so on. The line above is the sum of every dose taken so far, each fading from the moment it was given. That is why it climbs and settles instead of resetting between doses.

Peak to trough at steady state is 2 to the power of (time between doses divided by half-life). A short gap against a long half-life keeps the peak and trough close together. A long gap against a short half-life spreads them apart.

Time to steady state is about five half-lives, whatever the dose or gap. By then about 97% of the eventual plateau has been reached.
