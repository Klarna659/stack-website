How the times are worked out

Every N hours. Start at the first time and keep adding the gap. The list stops when the next time would be 24 hours or more after the first. Eight hours from 6:00 am gives 6:00 am, 2:00 pm and 10:00 pm, and then 6:00 am again tomorrow.

Every N days. Add the gap to the first date, again and again. Dates are counted on the calendar, so months, years and leap days roll over correctly. Add a time of day if you want one shown with each date.

N times a day. The first time is your wake time and the last is your bed time. The rest are spaced evenly in between. The gap is (bed time minus wake time) divided by (N minus 1). Three times between 7:00 am and 11:00 pm is 16 hours divided by 2, so 8 hours apart. One time a day is placed at your wake time. If your bed time is earlier than your wake time, it is read as the next morning, which suits night shifts.

This is not a dose recommendation. Use the times your pharmacist or clinician gave you. This page only turns a schedule you already have into clock times and dates.

Questions

Is every 8 hours the same as three times a day?

Not always. Every 8 hours is a fixed gap around the clock, so one time falls in the night. Three times a day is usually spread across the waking day, so the overnight gap is longer. Use the wording your pharmacist or clinician gave you, and check with them if you are unsure which one they meant.

What if the gap does not divide 24 hours evenly?

Then the times move from one day to the next. Every 5 hours from 8:00 am gives 8:00 am, 1:00 pm, 6:00 pm, 11:00 pm and 4:00 am, and the next one is 9:00 am the following day. The calculator says so when this happens.

Does it handle time zones or clock changes?

No. It works in plain clock times. If you are travelling, see schedules and time zones for what a schedule does when the clock changes.

Does this tell me when to take something?

No. It only does the arithmetic on a schedule you already have. Use the times your pharmacist or clinician gave you.

Not medical advice. This page does arithmetic only. It does not say how much to take, when to take it or what a result means. Talk to your doctor or pharmacist about your treatment.
